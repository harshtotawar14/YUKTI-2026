03 — LOCKED SERVICE WORKFLOW

Customer control is built into the service chain.

A booking does not jump from matching straight to payment. The worker can inspect, the customer approves price, service start is booking-linked, and completion is customer-confirmed before the final bill.

Problem / Photo→ Eligible Recommended Workers→ Customer Selects→ Worker Accepts→ Call / Inspection→ Estimate→ Customer Approval→ QR-Verified Start→ Service→ Worker Marks Complete→ Customer Confirms→ Itemized Bill→ Payment→ Invoice→ Rating / Feedback
No forced worker assignmentThe customer chooses from suitable workers; the selected worker still accepts or rejects.
No silent price increaseAny new work or cost requires a revised estimate and fresh customer approval.
No unilateral completionWorker marks complete, customer confirms, then the final bill is generated.
04 — FOUR CONNECTED ROLES

Clear responsibility at every handoff.

The cooperative creates the trusted operating environment; it does not manually control every normal booking. Federation remains primarily a network-governance and exception layer.

CUSTOMER

Choose, approve, verify, confirm

  • Create service request
  • Select a suitable worker
  • Approve original/revised estimate
  • Confirm service start and completion
  • Pay, receive invoice and rate
WORKER

Accept, inspect, estimate, deliver

  • Receive suitable opportunities
  • Accept or reject without forced assignment
  • Inspect and create itemized estimate
  • Deliver approved scope
  • View feedback and trust history
COOPERATIVE ADMIN

Verify, govern and strengthen capacity

  • Worker verification and membership
  • Booking and SLA visibility
  • Complaint ownership and audit
  • Trust / Green Tick review
  • Capacity and skill-gap action
FEDERATION / NETWORK

Coordinate by exception

  • Cross-cooperative policy
  • Regional capacity intelligence
  • Escalated dispute visibility
  • Network-level planning
  • No approval bottleneck on every job

06 — FIELD RESEARCH → DESIGN DECISION

Evidence is useful only when it changes the product.

SanPaid’s field interaction was used qualitatively: operational findings were mapped to product decisions, while unsupported national or government-approval claims were deliberately avoided.

SOURCE / FINDINGNearby reliable worker discovery is difficult

Field discussion around local service discovery and availability.

PRODUCT RESPONSEEligibility Gate + local recommended workers

Configurable local preference rather than a fixed national radius.

DESIGN DECISIONCustomer sees suitable verified choices; worker still accepts.
SOURCE / FINDINGService price varies with job scope

Different rates and diagnosis needs can create disputes.

PRODUCT RESPONSEEstimate + approval + revision trail

Approved charges become the source of the final itemized bill.

DESIGN DECISIONNo unapproved extra charge enters the final bill.
SOURCE / FINDINGUnknown in-home worker creates a safety concern

Identity and verification controls matter in household service delivery.

PRODUCT RESPONSEVerified profile + booking-specific service-start check

Customer confirms the assigned worker for the active booking.

DESIGN DECISIONTrust is layered, not treated as a one-time badge.
SOURCE / FINDINGIdle capacity can exist where another area has shortage

Cross-society sharing is plausible, but governance matters.

PRODUCT RESPONSECooperative Capacity Exchange

Worker consent + authorization + assignment record.

DESIGN DECISIONCapacity is coordinated without dissolving cooperative ownership.
